MY PHILOSOPHY

A personal perspective about life, death, perfection, strength, depth and adaptation.

THE PHILOSOPHY OF ANAS AYMAN

We are all, my friend, swimmers in the sea of life, looking at the sun. Each one of us believes that if he keeps swimming, one day he will reach the sun — perfection.

But the truth is that your destiny is trapped between two possibilities. A shark may eat you, or you may continue swimming with the certainty that you will eventually reach the sun.

The more you swim, the more you realize that you are not actually reaching it. You can see the sun getting closer, but eventually the distance between you and the sun becomes fixed.

Your arms become tired, and eventually you drown because you can no longer swim. In both cases, death is the ending.

And this is the meaning of life. The meaning of life is represented in the end of life itself — death.

The only one capable of defeating life and reaching the sun is the Creator of life Himself — God. He is the only one who possesses perfection.

We are all dead in the sea of life. Our only goal is to reach the closest possible distance to perfection and idealism.

BE WATER

The second part of my philosophy is that you must be like water or like a lizard.

When a fish leaves the sea, it dies because it cannot adapt. A lizard takes the color of the surface it stands on.

So be a lizard, not a fish.

At the same time, being like water is extremely important. Water changes its shape from one container to another.

It can be a bottle, a cooler or a cup. It does not say no.

Water gives you life when you drink it, but it can drown you if you underestimate its value.

And when water becomes angry, you can see a massive tsunami striking with enormous force.

All of this is H₂O.

Be water, not a lizard. Because water can also drown the lizard.

My philosophy combines intrinsic value, danger, strength, depth and adaptability. I combined ideas inspired by Nietzsche, Sartre and Bruce Lee and merged them into my own personal philosophy: The Philosophy of Anas Ayman.

TWO HALVES

My philosophy is divided into two halves. The first is finding the meaning of life — which is death, or the end of life itself.

The second is my philosophy while I am alive. I will die holding this perspective. I will teach this philosophy to my children.

And I challenge the world to produce a philosophy like mine.
