04 — Intellectual Work

Philosophy

Philosophy is an important component of the Anas Ayman identity. His personal perspective explores the relationship between life, mortality, adaptation, strength, individuality and the pursuit of perfection.

The Meaning of Life

We are all, in essence, swimmers in the sea of life, looking toward the sun. Each of us carries the conviction that by continuing to swim, we will eventually reach that sun — the symbol of perfection.

Yet our destiny appears to exist between two possibilities: we may be consumed by the dangers surrounding us, or we may continue swimming with the certainty that we will eventually arrive. The farther we swim, however, the more we may discover that the distance does not truly disappear.

Eventually, exhaustion becomes inevitable. In either case, death remains the final destination of life. From this perspective, the meaning of life can be found within life’s own conclusion: death.

Death represents a transition toward a greater existence beyond ordinary life. The only being capable of transcending life and reaching absolute perfection is the Creator of life Himself: God.

We are all, metaphorically, the dead within the sea of life. Our objective is not necessarily to achieve absolute perfection, but to move as close to it as possible.

Water, Adaptation and Strength

The second principle of my philosophy is the importance of becoming adaptable — like water, and in another sense, like a lizard.

A fish removed from the sea cannot survive because it cannot adapt to its new environment. A lizard, however, can adapt its appearance to its surroundings.

Water possesses another important quality: it changes its form according to the container that holds it. It can exist within a bottle, a glass or another vessel without losing its essential nature.

Water also gives life when respected and consumed properly, yet it possesses immense destructive power when underestimated. The same element that sustains life can become a force capable of overwhelming everything in its path.

My philosophy therefore attempts to combine personal value, danger, strength, depth and adaptability into a single perspective.

It draws inspiration from philosophical and cultural ideas associated with figures such as Nietzsche, Sartre and Bruce Lee, while developing them into my own personal framework: the philosophy of Anas Ayman.

My philosophy is divided into two fundamental parts.

The first is the search for the meaning of life through the recognition of mortality and the inevitability of death.

The second is the philosophy by which I intend to live: maintaining personal value, developing strength, remaining adaptable and continuing to pursue excellence.
