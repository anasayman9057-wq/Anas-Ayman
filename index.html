03 — THE PHILOSOPHY OF ANAS AYMAN

The Sea.
The Sun.
The Human.

ADAPTABILITY

Be The Lizard.

Adapt without losing yourself.

PERFECTION

Swim Toward The Sun.

Keep moving toward the closest possible point of perfection.

POWER

Become The Wave.

When necessary, move with the force of the ocean.

FLEXIBILITY

Be Water.

Change your form without losing your essence.

"We are all, my friend, swimmers in the sea of life, looking toward the sun."
We are all, my friend, in short, swimmers in the sea of life, looking toward the sun, and every one of us has the certainty that if we keep swimming, we will reach the sun one day — that is, perfection.

But the truth is that your destiny is confined between two possibilities: either a shark eats you, or you continue swimming and swimming with the certainty that you will reach the sun.

Yet the more you swim, you will discover that you are not actually reaching it, even though you can see that the sun appears to be getting closer.

You will continue swimming until the distance between you and the sun becomes fixed — neither increasing nor decreasing — until your arms become exhausted and you drown because you can no longer swim.

In both cases, death is the ending.

And this is the meaning of life.

The meaning of life is represented in the ending of life itself: death.

Because death carries you to a realm greater than life itself.

The only one capable of conquering life and reaching the sun is the Creator of life Himself: God.

He is the only one who possesses perfection.

We are all dead in the sea of life. Our only goal is to reach the closest possible distance to perfection and idealism.

This will remain my view of life until the day I die.
The second part of my philosophy is that you must be like water or like a lizard.

When a fish leaves the sea, it dies because it does not know how to adapt.

A lizard changes its color according to the surface on which it stands.

So be a lizard, not a fish.

At the same time, being like water is extremely important.

Water changes its shape from one container to another.

It can become a bottle, a cooler, a cup.

It does not say no.

At the same time, water gives you life when you drink it, and it can drown you if you underestimate its value and enter its depths while swimming.

And when water becomes angry, you see a massive tsunami crashing against its opponent with enormous force.

All of that is still simply: H₂O.

Be water.

Be adaptable.

Be powerful.

Be dangerous when necessary.

Be deep.
My philosophy combines self-worth, danger, strength, depth and adaptability.

I combined influences that I associate with Nietzsche, Sartre and Bruce Lee, and integrated them into my own personal philosophy:

The Philosophy of Anas Ayman.

Whenever someone tries to reduce your value, remember the sound of the waves.

Imagine the sound of a tsunami.

When you face resistance, move with the force of the waves.
My philosophy is divided into two halves.

The first half: finding meaning in life through the realization of death — the ending of life itself.

The second half: my philosophy while I am alive.

I will live and die holding this perspective.

I will teach this philosophy to my children.

And I challenge the world to create a philosophy like mine.
