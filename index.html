03 — PHILOSOPHY

My Philosophy

THE PHILOSOPHY OF ANAS AYMAN
PART I — THE MEANING OF LIFE
"We are all swimmers in the sea of life, looking toward the sun."

We are all, my friend, simply swimmers in the sea of life, looking toward the sun. Every one of us carries the certainty that if we keep swimming, one day we will reach the sun — the sun being perfection.

But the truth is that your destiny is ultimately confined between two possibilities. A shark may eat you, or you may continue swimming and swimming, convinced that you will eventually reach the sun.

Yet the more you swim, the more you realize that you are not actually reaching it. You can see the sun getting closer, but eventually the distance between you and the sun becomes fixed — neither increasing nor decreasing.

You keep swimming until your arms become exhausted, and eventually you drown because you no longer have the strength to swim.

In both cases, death is the ending.

And this is where the meaning of life begins.

The meaning of life is represented in the ending of life itself — death. Death transfers you to a realm greater than life itself.

The only one capable of conquering life and reaching the sun is the Creator of life Himself — God.

He is the only one who possesses absolute perfection.

We are all dead in the sea of life. Our only goal is to reach the closest possible distance to perfection.

This will remain my view of life until the day I die.

PART II — HOW I LIVE
"Be like water — or like the lizard."

The second part of my philosophy is that you must be like water — or like a lizard.

What do I mean?

When a fish leaves the sea, it dies. It does not know how to adapt.

The lizard, however, can take the color of the surface it stands on. It adapts to its surroundings.

So be like the lizard, not the fish. Learn how to adapt.

At the same time, being like water is extremely important.

Water changes its shape from one container to another.

It can be a bottle. It can be a cooler. It can be a glass.

Water does not say no. It takes the shape of what contains it without losing what it fundamentally is.

At the same time, water gives you life when you drink it.

But if you underestimate its value and enter its depths while swimming, it can drown you.

And when water becomes angry, you can see an enormous tsunami striking with tremendous force.

A tsunami does not ask its opponent for permission. It arrives with overwhelming power.

All of this is simply H₂O.

"Be water, not merely a lizard."

Water can even drown the lizard.

My philosophy combines self-worth, danger, strength, depth, and the ability to adapt.

I have taken influences from Nietzsche, Sartre, and Bruce Lee, and combined these ideas into something personal: my own philosophy.

This is the philosophy of Anas Ayman.

I do not believe that I should spend my life allowing people to reduce my value.

When someone underestimates you, remember the sound of the waves.

Imagine the sound of a tsunami striking. Remember that power.

When the moment comes to fight back, strike with the force of the waves. Strike with the force of the tsunami.

Let that sound remain in your mind: the sound of waves moving toward something that underestimated their power.

THE TWO HALVES

My philosophy is divided into two halves.

The first half is about finding meaning in life.

And that meaning is found in the ending of life itself — death.

The second half is about my philosophy while I am alive.

I will live and eventually die holding this perspective.

I will teach this philosophy to my children.

And I challenge the world to produce a philosophy like mine.
