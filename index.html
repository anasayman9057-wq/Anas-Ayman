04 / INTELLECTUAL WORK

Personal Philosophy

Philosophy constitutes an independent intellectual component of the Anas Ayman identity. It explores mortality, meaning, perfection, resilience, adaptability, personal value, strength and the relationship between the individual and the circumstances surrounding him.

The Meaning of Life

We are all, in essence, swimmers in the sea of life, looking toward the sun. Each of us carries the conviction that, by continuing to swim, we will eventually reach the sun — an image representing perfection.

Yet our destiny appears to be contained between two possibilities. A shark may consume us, or we may continue swimming with the certainty that the sun is within reach. The further we swim, however, the more we recognize that we are not actually arriving. Although the sun appears to become closer, eventually the distance between us and it reaches a point of stability.

Our arms eventually become exhausted. We can no longer continue swimming, and we drown.

In either circumstance, death remains the conclusion.

This is where I find the meaning of life: in the ending of life itself — death. Death represents a transition beyond the limitations of ordinary existence and toward something greater than life itself.

The only being capable of overcoming life and reaching absolute perfection is the Creator of life Himself: Allah.

He alone possesses complete perfection.

We are all, ultimately, mortal swimmers in the sea of life. Our objective is therefore not necessarily to achieve absolute perfection, but to approach it as closely as possible.

This will remain my perspective on life for as long as I live.



Adaptability, Strength and Identity

The second principle of my philosophy is the necessity of adaptability.

I believe a person should possess the flexibility of water and the adaptability represented by a lizard.

A fish depends upon the sea. When it leaves the water, it cannot survive. A lizard, however, adapts to its surroundings and changes its appearance according to the surface upon which it stands.

Therefore, I believe we should develop the ability to adapt rather than remain completely dependent upon a single environment.

Water provides another powerful metaphor.

Water changes its form according to the container that holds it. It may become a bottle, a cup or another vessel. It does not resist its environment simply because its surroundings have changed.

At the same time, water possesses enormous power.

It sustains life when respected and consumed responsibly, yet it can become dangerous when its depth and force are underestimated.

When its force reaches an extreme, water can become a tsunami — powerful enough to destroy what stands against it.

This philosophy therefore combines personal value, danger, strength, depth and adaptability.

It is influenced by philosophical ideas associated with thinkers such as Nietzsche and Sartre, while also drawing inspiration from Bruce Lee's understanding of adaptability and fluidity.

These influences are integrated into what I consider my personal philosophy: the philosophy of Anas Ayman.

My philosophy is divided into two fundamental parts.

The first part is the search for meaning in life, expressed through the inevitability of death and the conclusion of existence.

The second part concerns how I choose to live while I am alive — through resilience, adaptability, personal value, strength and continuous pursuit of improvement.

This perspective is intended to remain an essential part of my intellectual identity throughout my life.
